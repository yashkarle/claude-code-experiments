          Common Errors to Avoid

          Over-extension

          Preparing beyond the caries or beyond the necessary outline wastes tooth structure and weakens cusps.

          Under-cutting the Adjacent Tooth

          Damaging the mesial surface of the adjacent tooth during proximal box preparation. Use a matrix band as a guard.

          Pulp Exposure

          Preparing too deep on the pulpal floor. Use a periodontal probe to check depth (1.5–2mm from cavosurface).

          Unsupported Enamel

          Leaving enamel without dentine support, especially at the gingival margin. Will fracture under load.

          Incorrect Isthmus Width

          Too narrow = insufficient access; too wide = unnecessary weakening of cusps. Aim for 1/4 to 1/3 intercuspal distance.

          Rough Margins

          Irregular cavosurface margins lead to poor seal and microleakage. Use hand instruments to refine.

          Bevelling the Proximal Box

          Do NOT bevel the proximal box for composite — only bevel occlusal margins if needed. This is a common mistake.

          Forgetting Retention Form

          For composite, mechanical retention is NOT required (it bonds). Avoid placing undercuts unnecessarily.
