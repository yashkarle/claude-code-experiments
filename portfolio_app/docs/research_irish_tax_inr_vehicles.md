# Research: Irish tax on INR vehicles (2026-10-20)

Desk research in place of an adviser meeting. Not formal tax advice. Key claims were
spot-checked against Revenue's Tax and Duty Manual (TDM) Part 27-02-01 and Budget 2027
coverage.

## Verdict

**"Indian ETFs/MFs are more tax-efficient than EU ETFs" is wrong** for an Irish-resident
40% taxpayer. Indian MFs **and** NSE-listed ETFs are the *least* efficient vehicle
available. **Indian direct shares** (including a smallcase) are the efficient way to
hold INR equity.

## After-tax outcome on a €10,000 gain (sold 2027, exemption already used)

| Vehicle | Irish treatment | Rate | Net | Loss relief |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Indian MF | Offshore fund outside EU/EEA/OECD; Case IV income (s.745 / Sch.20) | ~52.35% (40 + 8 USC + 4.35 PRSI) | €4,765 | No |
| Indian NSE ETF (e.g. Nifty BeES) | Same. TDM: an ETF "would generally represent a material interest in an offshore fund" | ~52.35% | €4,765 | No |
| EU UCITS ETF | Exit tax; 8-year deemed disposal | 35% from 1 Jan 2027 (38% in 2026) | €6,500 | No |
| Indian direct shares / smallcase | Shares, not a fund (TDM example: quoted Indian company); CGT. Indian LTCG credited | 31% from 7 Oct 2026 | €6,900 | Yes, carried forward |
| EU/US direct shares | CGT | 31% | €6,900 | Yes |
| UK investment trust | Most likely shares (CGT). TDM's ISA example says otherwise; unresolved | 31% (worst case ~35%) | €6,900 | Yes, if CGT |
| Pension AVC | 40% relief in, tax-free growth | — | Best | — |

Other points:
- **Indian MFs/ETFs:** no 8-year deemed disposal, but there is a deemed disposal on
  death. Distributions are Case III income. Holding one makes you a **chargeable person**
  (s.896(5)), so you must file a Form 11.
- **India–Ireland DTAA:**
  - Gains on Indian shares: India may tax (Art. 13(5)), and Ireland credits that tax.
  - MF/ETF units probably fall under Art. 13(6), "taxable only in Ireland". A Mumbai
    tribunal accepted this for identically worded Singapore-treaty MF units (Anushka
    Shah, 2025).
  - So Indian TDS on MF units may not be creditable in Ireland. You would reclaim it
    from India with a Tax Residency Certificate and Form 10F.
- **FX trap on direct shares:** Indian tax is computed in INR and Irish tax in EUR. A
  weaker rupee can make the INR gain bigger than the EUR gain, and the excess Indian
  tax isn't creditable.
- **Naturalisation:**
  - Domicile depends on intention, not passport. Naturalisation is strong evidence of
    an Irish domicile of choice.
  - If you are non-domiciled today, the remittance basis may still shelter foreign
    gains not brought into Ireland. It is unclear whether this covers Case IV
    offshore-fund gains.
  - Becoming domiciled ends the CAT shield on foreign gifts and inheritances.
  - OCI holders can keep investing via NRE accounts after a KYC update. Kuvera and
    Zerodha OCI onboarding is unverified.
- **Budget 2027 (6 Oct 2026):**
  - CGT 33% → 31% from 7 Oct 2026.
  - Exit tax 38% → 35% from 1 Jan 2027.
  - **Savings & Investment Account from 1 Jul 2027:** €12k a year contribution cap, no
    CGT or exit tax inside it, 1% annual levy on value above €50k. Expected to allow
    shares, bonds and ETFs.

## Recommendations adopted

1. **No new money into Indian MFs or ETFs.** New INR money goes into direct-share
   baskets on Zerodha (smallcase or self-built).
2. **Hold the existing Indian MF/ETF positions.** Selling triggers ~52% Irish tax on the
   gain, and holding defers it. Only consider a sell-and-reinvest reset before
   naturalisation if a professional confirms you are currently non-domiciled.
3. **INR share of new money ≈ 20%**, as a deliberate diversifier, not a tax play.
4. **Revisit on 1 Jul 2027** when the Savings & Investment Account opens. It could make
   EU ETFs the best liquid vehicle (up to €12k a year each).
5. **Compliance:** declare the existing Indian MF/ETF holdings, and any 2025 disposals,
   on Form 11.

## Still uncertain

- Whether Revenue treats UK investment trusts as offshore funds (the ISA example in TDM 27-02-01).
- Whether the remittance basis covers Case IV offshore-fund gains.
- India's acceptance of Art. 13(6) for MF/ETF units under the Ireland treaty, and Irish credit for Indian TDS.
- Current domicile and the effect of naturalisation.
- Final Finance (No. 2) Bill 2026 detail: SIA eligibility, the 35% rate, CAT thresholds.
